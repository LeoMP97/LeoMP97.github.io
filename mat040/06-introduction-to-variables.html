You already use algebra every day. You might figure out how much to tip a server, calculate the change you should get when you pay for something, or compare batting averages. In this section we explore the words used to describe algebra.

Variables and Constants

Greg and Alex have the same birthday but were born in different years. This year Greg is 202020 and Alex is 232323, so Alex is 333 years older. When Greg was 121212, Alex was 151515. When Greg is 353535, Alex will be 383838. No matter what Greg’s age is, Alex’s age is always 333 more.

In the language of algebra, we say that Greg’s age and Alex’s age are variable, and the 333 is a constant. The ages change, or vary, but the 333 years between them always stays the same.

In algebra, letters of the alphabet are used to represent variables. If we call Greg’s age ggg, then Alex’s age is g+3g+3g+3.

A variable is a letter that represents a number or quantity whose value may change.
A constant is a number whose value always stays the same.

Letters often used for variables are xxx, yyy, aaa, bbb and ccc.
